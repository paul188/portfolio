## Overview

As **Co-Lecturer** at the Universität Bonn, Institut für Informatik 4, I research and teach **Fully Homomorphic Encryption (FHE)** and related privacy-preserving methods for sensitive medical data. The lecture ties the theory to neuroimaging data infrastructure, the domain I work in as a PhD candidate.

Students complete two practical labs in Python, built from Docker-based microservices that mirror production healthcare data platforms.

---

## Application in Medicine / Neuroimaging

Hospitals must share patient data for research while complying with DSGVO/GDPR. FHE enables a three-party protocol for this, modelled on [NeuroDaRe](/work/neurodare), the healthcare data-trust platform I built:

1. The **Researcher** sends a research query to **DaRe** (the Data Trust / Treuhaender).
2. The **Hospital** sends MRI scan data and patient demographics, encrypted with its own key <InlineMath math="k" /> as <InlineMath math="\text{Enc}_k(\text{PHI})" />. Raw PHI never leaves the hospital.
3. DaRe runs the **FHE computation** on the ciphertext: <InlineMath math="f(\text{Enc}_k(\text{PHI})) = \text{Enc}_k(f(\text{PHI}))" />. DaRe holds no key, so it has **no PHI access**.
4. The Hospital **decrypts with key <InlineMath math="k" />** and, after institutional approval, releases the plaintext result to the Researcher.

## What is Fully Homomorphic Encryption?

FHE lets a server compute directly on ciphertext, for **any** function <InlineMath math="f" />. The decrypted result equals the plaintext computation:

<BlockMath math="\text{Dec}\!\left(f\!\left(\text{Enc}(x)\right)\right) = f(x)" />

## Lab 1 — FHE Correlation Analysis

The lab computes the correlation between **age** and **brain volume** under homomorphic encryption. A data requester gets the statistic while patient-level data stays protected. The prototype runs on a small synthetic dataset.

### The Hard Part: Division and Square Roots

The Pearson correlation coefficient between <InlineMath math="X" /> (age) and <InlineMath math="Y" /> (brain volume) over <InlineMath math="n" /> observations is:

<BlockMath math="r = \frac{n\sum_{i=1}^n x_i y_i - \sum_{i=1}^n x_i \cdot \sum_{i=1}^n y_i}{\sqrt{\!\left(n\sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2 - \Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^n x_i\Bigr)^{\!2}\right) \cdot \left(n\sum_{i=1}^n y_i^2 - \Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^n y_i\Bigr)^{\!2}\right)}}" />

The sums of products are native to FHE, which operates over **polynomial rings**. Division and square roots have no closed polynomial form and must be approximated. Each multiplicative level consumes noise budget, and without bootstrapping, circuits of depth > 10–20 become infeasible.

**This lab computes the full correlation entirely under FHE**, using three strategies:

**Strategy 1: Newton–Raphson for the reciprocal.** Quadratic convergence means 3–5 iterations suffice for correlation precision.

<BlockMath math="x_{k+1} = x_k\,(2 - a\,x_k) \;\xrightarrow{\;k\to\infty\;}\; 1/a" />

**Strategy 2: Newton–Raphson for the inverse square root.** Then <InlineMath math="r = N \cdot y_\infty" /> is a single ciphertext multiplication.

<BlockMath math="y_{k+1} = \frac{y_k}{2}\!\left(3 - D\,y_k^2\right), \quad D = D_1 D_2 \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; y \to \frac{1}{\sqrt{D}}" />

**Strategy 3: Chebyshev polynomial approximation** on a bounded interval <InlineMath math="[a,b]" />, known from domain bounds on age and brain volume.

<BlockMath math="g(x) \approx \sum_{k=0}^{d} c_k\,T_k(x), \quad T_{k+1}(x) = 2x\,T_k(x) - T_{k-1}(x)" />

All three use only multiplications and additions. The **CKKS scheme** (via OpenFHE) rescales after each multiplication automatically.

## Lab 2 — Private Set Intersection

The goal is a **secure way for hospitals to determine which of their patients were also treated by other hospitals**, under these constraints:

- Outsiders who gain access to the prepared patient records cannot derive information about individual patients
- Each hospital learns **only** which of its own patients were treated by the other hospitals
- Computation and storage can be **outsourced to a Cloud Provider** without revealing patient data to that provider
- The system is efficient and scalable, and needs **no multi-party computation or shared secrets** between hospitals

### Why Not FHE for Set Intersection?

Set intersection only asks whether two values are the same. An encrypted equality check in FHE needs a polynomial approximation of the indicator function, with high **multiplicative depth** and a computation time **10³–10⁶×** that of a plaintext check.

**Hashing is far more efficient.** The lab uses Argon2 for its **memory-hardness**, which resists brute-force and GPU attacks on the hashed patient identifiers. No raw PII reaches the server, and the intersection is computed in near-linear time.
