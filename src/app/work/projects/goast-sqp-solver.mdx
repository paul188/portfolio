## Overview

This Master's thesis in Mathematics (University of Bonn, Institute for Numerical Simulation) asks: given a thin elastic plate that can fold along prescribed edges, which fold geometry minimises the elastic bending energy?

The thesis derives and implements a custom **Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP)** solver, extending the [GOAST](https://gitlab.com/numod/goast) geometric optimisation library.

---

## Mathematical Formulation

The plate is modelled as a 2D triangular mesh embedded in <InlineMath math="\mathbb{R}^3" />, deformed by a map <InlineMath math="\varphi" />. The discrete bending energy sums over the mesh edges:

<BlockMath math="W_B = \sum_{e \in E} \|e\| \cdot \frac{(\tilde{\Theta}_e - \Theta_e)^2}{h_e}" />

where <InlineMath math="\Theta_e" /> and <InlineMath math="\tilde{\Theta}_e" /> are the dihedral angles at edge <InlineMath math="e" /> in the reference and deformed configurations, <InlineMath math="\|e\|" /> is the edge length, and <InlineMath math="h_e" /> is the average incident triangle height.

To optimise a parametrised family of fold geometries, the thesis solves a nonlinear equality-constrained problem:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \min_{t,\, \varphi} \quad & C[\varphi] \\ \text{subject to} \quad & \nabla_\varphi W[\psi(t),\, \varphi] = 0 \end{aligned}" />

Here <InlineMath math="t" /> is the fold parameter and <InlineMath math="\psi(t)" /> the reference configuration it defines. The constraint requires <InlineMath math="\varphi" /> to be a physical equilibrium of the plate. It is itself the solution of a high-dimensional variational equation, which makes standard optimisation impractical.

---

## SQP Algorithm

Each iteration linearises the constraint and solves a local quadratic subproblem:

<BlockMath math="\begin{aligned} \min_{p} \quad & f(x_k) + \nabla f(x_k)^\top p + \tfrac{1}{2}\, p^\top B_k\, p \\ \text{s.t.} \quad & c(x_k) + \nabla c(x_k)^\top p = 0 \end{aligned}" />

where <InlineMath math="B_k" /> approximates the Hessian of the Lagrangian. The solver adds four components:

- **BFGS quasi-Newton Hessian.** Third-order derivatives of <InlineMath math="W" /> are unavailable in closed form, so a BFGS approximation replaces the exact Hessian. To prevent numerical drift, <InlineMath math="B_k" /> is reinitialised to the identity every 20 iterations.
- **Merit function with Armijo line search.** An <InlineMath math="\ell_1" /> merit function accepts or rejects each candidate step. Its penalty weight adapts automatically so that the SQP step is always a descent direction.
- **Second-order correction.** Near the solution, a correction step compensates for linearisation error.
- **Schur complement.** The KKT system at each iteration has size <InlineMath math="O(6n + 2m)" /> (<InlineMath math="n" /> = vertices, <InlineMath math="m" /> = fold DOFs). The Schur complement <InlineMath math="J = \nabla c \, B_k^{-1} \nabla c^\top" /> (size <InlineMath math="O(3n + m)" />) is factored once via LU decomposition and reused, giving an **8× asymptotic cost reduction**.

The method achieves **superlinear convergence** to KKT points under second-order sufficient conditions, and the merit function with Armijo backtracking guarantees global convergence.

## Results

**Convergence in practice.** All case studies show the superlinear convergence predicted by theory. The solver reaches machine-precision constraint satisfaction within a small number of outer SQP iterations. The line search only backtracks in early iterations.

**Invariance of optimal fold patterns.** The optimal fold geometry appears to be invariant under changes in the fold parametrisation. Substantially different choices of the map <InlineMath math="t \mapsto \psi(t)" /> converge to fold patterns that are geometrically indistinguishable. This suggests that the optimal fold is a property of the energy itself.
