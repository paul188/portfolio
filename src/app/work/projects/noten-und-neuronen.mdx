## Overview

**Noten und Neuronen** is a science concert series on music's preventive effects against neurodegenerative diseases. Concerts across Germany combine live orchestra performances with audience research: mobile MRI scans, memory tests, and real-time interactive experiments during the intermissions.

As **Software Team Lead**, I designed and built the web platform from scratch: a real-time application that runs on participants' own smartphones during a live concert and scales to **2,500 simultaneous users**.

**Live app:** [noten-und-neuronen-dd60a.web.app](https://noten-und-neuronen-dd60a.web.app/)

## Concerts

The app has run live at four concerts of the 2026 series so far. "On the app" counts the phones that signed in during the concert; "Fun rating" is the audience's mean answer, from 1 to 5, to the post-concert question on how much fun the interactive experiments were.

## Architecture

The platform is built on **Vue 3 + Firebase**. Code splitting means participants only download the sub-app for their current phase.

- **Firestore**: session state, onboarding, phase orchestration
- **Realtime Database**: high-frequency writes for the Smartphone-Chor (600+ writes/s)
- **Cloud Functions** (TypeScript): session management, phase transitions
- **Vite PWA**: service worker caching for offline resilience and sub-second load times

## User View

<PhoneMockup />

Participants join via a QR code in the concert programme, with no login and no app download. Onboarding (age, musical background, consent) takes under two minutes. The phone then follows the current phase automatically. A wake-lock keeps it from sleeping, and the service worker carries the session through a brief Wi-Fi dropout.

## Evaluation and Takeaways

At the end of each evening, the app's Finale sub-app asked participants how much fun the interactive experiments were, on a scale from 1 (Schlecht) to 5 (Super!). Across all four concerts, **1,175 participants** answered, with a mean of **4.45** and a median of **5**; **91% answered positively** (4 or 5).

The load testing paid off: the first full-scale deployment, with over 1,000 real concurrent users on a congested venue network, behaved as the simulations predicted.

## Technical Challenges

The timeline was short and the concert date fixed. There was no soft launch: the first real test was 2,500 people in a hall. Two problems dominated the work:

- **Limited bandwidth in the concert hall.** A venue network cannot serve fresh assets to over 1,000 phones at once. **Cache pre-warming** during onboarding and **image preloading** during the waiting screens let the phones render each phase from local cache.
- **Thundering-herd spikes.** Everyone joins or changes phase at the same moment. Randomized **jitter** spreads it out: 0–5 s on startup to protect Firebase Auth, 0–1 s on phase transitions to smooth Firestore listener storms.

The Smartphone-Chor's pitch data runs on the **Realtime Database** (a 4× cheaper write rate than Firestore). I load-tested the system against all known Firebase limits before the concert and kept **Lighthouse P95 under 2 s** on congested mobile networks.
