## Overview

**Noten und Neuronen** is a science concert series exploring music's preventive effects against neurodegenerative diseases. Concerts across Germany combine live orchestra performances with audience participation research — mobile MRI scans, memory tests, and real-time interactive experiments during the intermissions.

As **Software Lead**, I designed and built the entire web platform from scratch: a high-concurrency, real-time application that runs on participants' own smartphones during a live concert, scaling reliably to **2,500 simultaneous users**.

**Live website:** [notenundneuronen.de](https://notenundneuronen.de) · **Live app:** [noten-und-neuronen-dd60a.web.app](https://noten-und-neuronen-dd60a.web.app/)

## The Concert: Kölner Philharmonie

In July 2026 the platform faced its audience: a sold-out Kölner Philharmonie with **2,500 visitors**, of whom **more than 1,000** joined the interactive experiments live on their own phones — sorting song timelines, rating musical passages, and turning the hall into a smartphone choir between the orchestra's pieces. The concert went off without a hitch: no downtime, no degraded phases, and the app kept pace with the live programme from onboarding to finale.

## Architecture

The platform is built on **Vue 3 + Firebase** with aggressive code splitting so participants only download the sub-app relevant to their current phase:

- **Firebase Firestore** — session state, onboarding, phase orchestration
- **Firebase Realtime Database** — high-frequency writes for the Smartphone-Chor (600+ writes/s)
- **Firebase Cloud Functions** (TypeScript) — session management, phase transitions
- **Vite PWA** — service worker caching for offline resilience and sub-second load times
- **Manual code chunks** — admin, each sub-app, and Firebase SDK in separate bundles

## User View

<PhoneMockup />

Participants join via a QR code in the concert programme — no login, no app download, and onboarding (age, musical background, consent) takes under two minutes. From there, the phone locks into the current phase automatically as the PI drives the timeline, with animated waiting screens between sub-apps, a wake-lock to keep phones from sleeping, and service-worker offline resilience so a brief Wi-Fi dropout doesn't drop the participant from the session.

The biggest takeaway: the load-testing discipline paid off. The first full-scale deployment — over 1,000 real concurrent users on a congested venue network — behaved exactly as the simulations predicted, and the experiments produced clean research data for the study team.

## Technical Challenges

Building the entire platform from scratch on a short development timeline, against an immovable concert date, was the project's defining constraint. There was no soft launch: the first real test was 2,500 people in a hall. Two problems dominated the engineering work:

- **Limited bandwidth in the concert hall.** A venue network cannot serve fresh assets to over 1,000 phones at once, so the app had to stop needing it: **cache pre-warming** during onboarding fills the service-worker cache with everything later phases require, and **image data is preloaded** during the waiting screens before each sub-app — by the time a phase starts, the phones render from local cache instead of hitting the network.
- **Thundering-herd spikes.** When everyone joins or transitions at the same moment, the backend sees one giant synchronized burst. Randomized **jitter** spreads it out: 0–5 s on startup to protect Firebase Auth, 0–1 s on phase transitions to smooth Firestore listener storms.

Beyond that, the Smartphone-Chor's high-frequency pitch data runs on the **Realtime Database** (a 4× cheaper write rate than Firestore), and the whole system was load-tested against all known Firebase limits before the concert — keeping **Lighthouse P95 under 2 s** on congested mobile networks.
